### What "SSD Program" means (know this cold — it signals you did homework)
Amazon's **Sub-Same-Day** network: hybrid buildings (~**220,000 sq ft** typical) that combine
fulfillment + sortation + last-mile delivery-station functions in one box to enable
faster-than-same-day delivery. Compared to a 1M+ sq ft mega-FC, SSD/delivery-station projects are:
- **Smaller and often retrofits/conversions** of existing tilt-up warehouses (e.g., a 202k sq ft
  Dublin, CA tilt-up renovation; a 145k sq ft Toledo build-to-suit with 12 docks and **1,074 van spaces**)
- Dominated by **van/employee parking fields, trailer courts, dock equipment, electrical service
  upgrades and EV charging at scale** (200–600+ chargers per site in recent retrofits — [Oltmans Amazon EV retrofit](https://www.oltmans.com/projects/amazon-ev-retrofit))
- Driven by a **fixed launch date** with Amazon Ops/launch teams as the internal customer
- Run **design-build**, with the CM coordinating the GC plus Amazon's own vendors (MHE/sort
  equipment, low-voltage, EV charging) inside the same schedule

This scale is actually a *good* match for your $5M warehouse experience — a delivery-station
retrofit is much closer to a $5M TI/retrofit than to a $300M mega-FC. Say that if asked about scale.

Related public roles for flavor: [Amazon.jobs — Construction Manager, North America](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/2928552/construction-manager-north-america-construction-manager) (permitting, new builds, retrofits, expansions; managing contracted resources; multiple simultaneous projects in various locations), [Actalent Construction Manager jobs](https://www.actalentservices.com/en/careers/engineering-careers/construction-manager-jobs).

## 3. Big-Warehouse Construction: War Stories & Domain Research (500k–1.5M sq ft)

Real published projects, postmortems, and recurring failure modes — fuel for making your answers
concrete. **These are other people's projects: use them as industry context ("the kind of issues
this asset class deals with…"), as detail for what you observed/planned on your own project, and
as proof you know the domain — don't claim them as your own.**

### 3.1 Published project stories

**Gray Construction — Amazon FC, Bessemer AL** ([Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/8-ways-warehouse-construction-has-evolved/560388/))
- $325M, **22 miles of conveyor**, 100k+ items/day capacity. Industry target: **1M sq ft in 12 months**; Layton built a 2M sq ft Macy's DC in 12 months.
- Power demand evolution: ~5,000-amp services historically → **~12,000 amps** for modern automated DCs.
- Site coverage flipped: building footprint now only **25–33% of the site** — trailer courts, van lots, and stormwater eat the rest (GE Appliances Denver: 100 trailer stalls, 70 dock doors, parking for 1,500).

**Terracon — 1M+ sq ft FC, Metro Air Park, Sacramento** ([Terracon](https://www.terracon.com/2021/10/05/meeting-the-challenges-of-fast-paced-distribution-center-construction/))
- Built on a former rice field → native soils needed **lime/cement chemical treatment** with field density testing.
- Pours up to **1,000 CY at once (~70 mixer trucks)**, two crews; **footings/slabs poured overnight, tilt panels cast during the day**.
- Schedule saver: **on-site concrete lab for early breaks** so panels could be lifted without waiting on off-site results; crews cross-certified in rebar + concrete to kill re-inspections; deliberate relationship with the county inspector.

**Hourigan — Deepwater Industrial Park, Richmond VA** ([Hourigan](https://www.hourigan.group/resources/insights/building-blocks-for-successful-warehouse-construction/))
- **1.5M sq ft on 110 acres**, 17 existing structures demolished. Crushed **106,770 tons of on-site concrete** for reuse as engineered fill — saved **$1.1M+** and the haul-off.

**Steel joist/deck crisis, 2021–22** ([Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/steel-roofing-materials-shortage-construction/607891/), [Eng-Tips thread](https://www.eng-tips.com/threads/joist-and-deck-shortages-updates.494194/), [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/steel-roofing-materials-shortage-construction/607866/))
- Bar joist lead times hit **10–14 months** (some quoted 2+ years); steel mill products **+123% YoY**; bids held for only **10 days**.
- Industry lore: a major online retailer (read: Amazon) bought up essentially the whole US joist supply through late 2021.
- Workarounds: **substituting wide-flange/plate girders for bar joists** (owner-approved redesign), early buyout, pre-purchasing steel into laydown yards.

**BD+C project roundup** ([BD+C](https://www.bdcnetwork.com/aec-firms-tap-boom-logistics-center-construction))
- Amazon Kansas City: **856,000 sq ft (2.3–2.4M usable with mezzanines)**, tilt-up + structural steel, delivered by Clayco in ~13 months; Fresno FC in ~11 months; one Oklahoma site went **vertical within 30 days of permit**.
- Amazon's usual GC bench: **Clayco, Ryan Companies, ARCO, Gray, Whiting-Turner, Conlan, Panattoni** — design-build, pre-engineered systems, GC as CM-of-record.

### 3.2 Recurring failure modes (the "what goes wrong" catalog)

| Failure mode | Realistic specifics | Standard mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Slab FF/FL & joint curl** | Spec FF 35/FL 25 random traffic; FF 50/FL 35 for tall rack/AGV. Floor **passes at 72-hr testing, then curls at joints** months later under MHE traffic ([Concrete Network](https://www.concretenetwork.com/industrial-floors/flatness.html), [Archtoolbox](https://www.archtoolbox.com/ff-and-fl-numbers/)) | Laser screed, subgrade prep, sawcut timing, joint fill timing, moisture testing before coatings |
| **Steel joist/deck lead time** | 10–14 mo leads, +45–123% pricing, 10-day bid validity (2021–22) | Early procurement/buyout before GMP; girder substitution; warehousing steel |
| **Unsuitable soils / earthwork** | Undercut + engineered fill at **$15–40/CY**; swell/shrink misestimates; lime/cement treatment | Early geotech, balanced-site grading, chemical stabilization |
| **ESFR water supply gap** | ESFR pumps ~4,500–6,000 L/min @ 1.0–1.5 MPa; weak municipal main forces **fire pump + storage tank** or in-rack combo; late rack layout changes trigger NFPA 13 recalc + AHJ re-approval ([QRFS](https://blog.qrfs.com/406-esfr-sprinklers-a-strong-line-of-defense-in-storage-applications/)) | Hydrant flow test at due diligence; freeze rack layout early |
| **Power / utility delays** | 12,000-amp services; **transformer lead times 6–10 months** ([Maddox](https://www.maddox.com/products-and-services/substation)) | Engage utility at site selection; order gear at design start; temp power bridge |
| **Tilt panel lifting** | Panels crack from undersized cranes, bad rigging, or lifting before strength ([Dayton Superior handbook](https://www.daytonsuperior.com/docs/default-source/handbooks/tilt-up-handbook.pdf)) | Early-break testing on site (see Terracon), insert/rigging design review |
| **Permitting / AHJ bottlenecks** | Fire marshal sign-off, inspector availability on compressed schedules | Relationship building, cross-certified inspectors/crews, pre-submittal meetings |
| **MHE/conveyor/racking vs shell GC** ⭐ | The most Amazon-relevant: GC must hold slab tolerances, embeds, conveyor penetrations and steel supports for the integrator; **late MHE layout changes ripple into sprinklers, power drops, and floor flatness** | Milestone-based "building ready" turnover states; weekly GC↔integrator coordination |
| **Schedule compression** | 9–13 months for ~1M sq ft; overlapping shell/interiors; night concrete + day tilt casting; 500–1,000 workers at peak | Fast-track with explicit design-freeze gates; multi-shift |

### 3.3 SSD / delivery-station specifics (your actual lane)
- Often **retrofits of existing 100–220k sq ft tilt-ups**, not greenfield — TI-scale budgets ($3–15M range), which is *exactly the scale of your $5M project*.
- Scope centers on: dock packages (levelers, seals, restraints), van staging + parking fields (1,000+ van spaces), **EV charging buildouts (200–600 chargers → big electrical service upgrades + utility coordination)**, interior sort equipment coordination, life-safety re-permitting in an existing shell.
- The constraint that rules everything: a **fixed launch date** owned by Amazon Ops — the building is "late" the moment any partner team (MHE install, IT, ops hiring) loses its window. This is exactly why the hiring manager emphasized *proactive communication when timelines shift*.

### Q2. "Tell me about a time you didn't think you'd meet a commitment — how did you communicate it?" (Earn Trust / Deliver Results — [Glassdoor verbatim](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Delivering-results-Q-Tell-me-about-a-time-when-you-took-on-something-significant-outside-your-area-of-responsibility-Why-QTN_3052842.htm))

### 5.2 Pharma-warehouse fluency (your project's domain — know these cold)

- **GMP storage rules govern everything**: cleanliness, segregation (quarantine vs released
  product cages), pest control, and documented temperature control ([Pharmaceutical Technology — GMP warehouse design](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/fundamentals-gmp-warehouse-design), [ASC — GMP for warehousing](https://ascsoftware.com/blog/gmp-for-warehousing/))
- **Temperature bands**: CRT **15–25°C** for most product; cold room **2–8°C** for biologics —
  Seagen's products (ADCs/antibody drugs) are cold-chain, so HVAC redundancy and monitoring matter
  ([Sensitech — pharma warehouse requirements](https://www.sensitech.com/en/blog/blog-articles/blog-pharmaceutical-warehouse-requirements.html))
- **Temperature mapping**: before occupancy the space gets mapped for hot/cold spots — sensors
  top-of-rack vs floor level (tall racking = big top-to-bottom variation), summer + winter studies,
  then **IQ/OQ/PQ qualification** of the space ([PharmOut — temperature mapping](https://www.pharmout.net/understanding-temperature-mapping/), [Eupry — mapping guidelines](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/guidelines-pharmaceutical/))
- **Why this impresses for Amazon**: a GMP warehouse TI is *harder* than a standard one — every
  system you turned over had to be validated, not just inspected. "My building didn't just need a
  C of O, it needed to pass qualification" is a strong Insist-on-Highest-Standards line.
