# Welcome to the Course

This is a 120-hour TEFL certificate course. It prepares you to teach English to speakers of other languages, whether you are heading to a classroom in another country, teaching online, or starting your first job at a language school. The course assumes no teaching experience. It does assume that you can read English comfortably, since you will be reading a great deal of it here.

The course has 14 modules and 63 units. A unit is one sitting of work: a reading of two to three thousand words, a few questions along the way, and a short quiz at the end. Most people finish a unit in about two hours. Work through the modules in order. Later units build on earlier ones, and the tests are designed around that sequence.

## What makes this course different

Two commitments shaped everything in it.

The first is honesty about evidence. Teaching is full of advice, and some of the most popular advice has no research behind it. When this course tells you that something works, it names the study that showed it, with the author and year in the text and the full reference at the end of the unit. When the research is contested, the course says so and gives you the more cautious number. When a popular idea has failed its tests, the course tells you that too. You will meet a unit on learning styles whose main finding is that the theory does not hold up (Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork, 2009). A teacher who knows the difference between evidence and folklore makes better decisions for her students.

The second is that the course practices what it teaches. Two findings from memory research run through its design. Retrieving something from memory strengthens it far more than reading it again: in one well-known experiment with foreign-language vocabulary, learners who kept testing themselves recalled about 80 percent of the words a week later, while learners who kept re-studying recalled about a third (Karpicke & Roediger, 2008). And practice spread out over time beats practice massed into one session (Kim & Webb, 2022). That is why every unit ends with a quiz, why every module test asks you two questions from earlier modules, and why the final exam reaches back across the whole course. The questions are the teaching. Treat every quiz as a chance to strengthen what you know rather than a hurdle to clear.

## The people you will meet

Examples in this course follow a small recurring cast of learners: Carlos from Mexico, Daniel from Brazil, Wei from China, Fatima from Morocco, Yuki from Japan, and Priya from India, taught by Ms. Reyes and Mr. Osei. You will watch these students mislearn the past tense, fall silent in discussions, surprise their teachers, and improve. Their first languages were chosen deliberately, because the errors a Spanish speaker makes in English differ from the errors a Mandarin speaker makes, and a good teacher learns to read those differences. By the end of the course you will know these six students well, and the situations you have watched them work through will be the situations you recognize in your own classroom.

## How the course is organized

- **Modules 1 to 3** cover the foundations: the world of TEFL, how people learn languages, and the English grammar a teacher needs to explain with confidence.
- **Modules 4 to 6** cover the craft of teaching language itself: methods, vocabulary, and pronunciation.
- **Modules 7 to 9** cover the four skills of reading, listening, speaking, and writing, and how to give feedback and correct errors.
- **Modules 10 to 13** cover the working classroom: planning lessons, managing a room full of people, understanding your learners, and assessment.
- **Module 14** covers specializations and your growth on the job, from teaching young learners to observing your peers.

## How assessment works

There are three layers, each with a purpose.

**Unit quizzes** end every unit: six questions, including one scenario that puts you in a classroom and asks what you would do. These are self-checks. Use them honestly, without looking back at the text on your first attempt, because the act of retrieving the answer is what makes it stick.

**Module tests** close every module. Section A tests the module you just finished, with three questions per unit. Section B asks two questions from earlier modules; forgetting some of it is expected, and the retrieval will restore it. Section C is a scenario worth five points. The pass mark is 70 percent. If you fall short, reread the units the missed questions came from and take the test again.

**The final exam** covers the whole course: one question from every unit, plus three extended scenarios that ask you to combine ideas across modules, the way real teaching does. The pass mark is 70 percent.

## A note on how this course treats you

The course was built from public materials of the United States Department of State's American English program, from published research in language teaching, and from the working knowledge of classroom practice. Every unit lists its sources. Nothing here asks you to take a claim on faith, and nothing here pads the hours with filler. The 120 hours are reading, thinking, and answering questions that make the material yours.

Start with Module 1, Unit 1. The students are waiting.
