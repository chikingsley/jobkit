# Full-course QA review (2026-07-17)

A complete pass over the shipped state of the course: all 63 units read in full, all 14 module
tests + final exam structurally audited, and every research citation verified against the
published literature. Method: seven parallel review passes (five covering the units by module
group, one on the assessment layer, one citation fact-check with web verification), plus
course-wide mechanical sweeps (cast greps, spelling, references, anchor checks).

## Resolution addendum (2026-07-16 takeover branch)

This document is the historical pre-repair review, not the status of
codex/tefl-course-takeover. The takeover pass resolved the enumerated ship-blockers and added
regression checks for the mechanical failure classes:

- The strict content audit now reports **0 errors and 0 warnings across 94 rendered files**.
  Cast role, age, identity, pronoun, artifact, out-of-cast-name, anchor, reference, American
  English, punctuation, and style checks are clean.
- The factual and doctrinal repairs include the EFL/ESL distinction, CLT intellectual history,
  the Kang/Goo evidence, selection bias, the General American phonology rewrite, the
  eight-form/twelve-label reconciliation, American present-perfect usage, Mandarin progressive
  marking, word families, bottom-up processing, recasts versus prompts, and the unsettled
  treatment-length evidence.
- The missing instructional content is now present: the canonical Willis task sequence and
  references in 4.4, correction codes and a worked coded draft in 8.4, a complete timed
  45-minute lesson plan in 10.3, and validity/reliability plus inter-rater safeguards in 13.1.
- Module-test answer positions are **A=50, B=51, C=50, D=49** across 200 MCQs. The correct
  option is strictly longest in **45/200 (22.5%)**, down from the 67.9% live baseline measured
  before option editing. Every scenario key now has explicit 0-5 or 0-10 score bands, repeated
  review stems are excluded across module tests, and all three final scenarios require
  cross-module synthesis.
- The audit now fails on answer-position bias, keyed-option-length bias above 40%, missing
  scenario rubrics, orphaned anchors, and the earlier content-consistency classes, so the
  highest-volume defects cannot silently return through ordinary regeneration.

One correction to the historical review itself: Goo et al. (2015) spans pages **443-482**, not
443-483. Its 34-study pool partially overlaps Norris and Ortega (11 shared, 23 newly included).

## Verdict

**Not ready to ship — but the foundations are genuinely good and the distance to shippable is
short.** The pedagogy is sound in all 63 units, the research citation layer is remarkably clean
(16/16 cited works are real; nearly every number verified against the actual papers), the
assessment layer is structurally perfect, and the writing at paragraph level is clear and
professional. What blocks launch is a set of concentrated, mostly mechanical defect classes —
the largest of which was *introduced by* the 2026-06-09 cast-consolidation pass rather than by
original generation. Nothing except unit 3.6 (and Module 6's vowel content) needs
regeneration-level work; everything else is enumerable line edits plus a few new gate checks.

Per-unit verdicts from the reviewers: **1 ship-ready** (8.3), **~28 minor-fixes**, **~34
needs-work**. No unit fails on teaching substance; every "needs work" is cast/anchor/reference/
consistency damage sitting on top of sound content.

## What is genuinely good (don't touch)

- **Citation integrity — the course's core marketing claim survives audit.** Of 16 distinct
  cited works across 13 citation-bearing units, zero are fabricated. Authors, journals, volumes,
  pages, and effect sizes check out: Karpicke & Roediger 2008 (80% vs ~33–36%), Bryfonski &
  McKay 2019 (52 studies, d=0.93), Xuan, Cheung & Liu 2025 (real, g=0.61), Norris & Ortega 2000,
  Spada & Tomita 2010, Goo et al. 2015, Lyster & Saito 2010 (15 studies, N=827), Li 2010,
  Nakanishi 2015 (d=0.46/0.71), Jeon & Day 2016 (d=0.57/0.79), Hamada 2020 (0.55→0.37),
  Nakata 2015, Kim & Webb 2022 (48 experiments, N=3,411), Pashler et al., Willingham et al.,
  Gregg 1984, Hall & Cook 2012. Contested findings are consistently presented with the
  conservative number, exactly as the intro promises. The exceptions are listed below — five
  fixes, all small.
- **Assessment structure is contract-perfect.** Every module test has exactly 3 Section A
  questions per unit with no unit skipped; Section B is exactly 2 genuine earlier-module
  retrieval items on every test after M1; point totals are arithmetically correct; the final
  covers all 63 units exactly once. All ~275 assessment anchor quotes trace verbatim to unit
  text, and extensive key sampling found **zero wrong keys**.
- **Grammar content in M2/M3 is largely accurate** — the English-internal rules, the tense/aspect
  analysis (2.3 especially), conditionals, reported speech, phrasal-verb separability all
  verified. The factual failures cluster narrowly in *cross-linguistic* claims (below).
- **Body word counts match the intro's promise** (2,569–3,356 words, mean ~2,977, vs the
  advertised 2,500–3,300).
- **Sourcing and licensing discipline** (docs/SOURCES.md) is careful and well documented.

## Ship-blockers

### 1. The cast remap broke character consistency course-wide (~150+ verified violations)

The 06-09 consolidation remapped names by gender only, without checking **role, age,
nationality, or L1**. Verified failure modes:

- **Learners used as teachers: 73 instances across 27 units** (`grep -rE "teacher (named|like|called) (Carlos|Daniel|Wei|Fatima|Yuki|Priya)"`). Worst: 14.4 uses learners as
  teachers *throughout the whole unit* while Ms. Reyes / Mr. Osei never appear; module 10 has 22
  instances; same-unit role flip-flops in 1.1, 10.3 (Fatima and Carlos each teacher in one
  paragraph, student in another).
- **Wrong ages:** Fatima (adult) as a six-year-old (10.5:21), a five-year-old (1.2:11), a
  seven-to-eight-year-old (12.2:41); Yuki (teenager) as a commuting adult professional (7.1:19,
  14.2:9) and as a teacher of adults (12.2:21, 4.5:33, 11.1:5); whole units (11.2, 14.1) place
  the adult cast in primary classrooms; module-01-test Q16 assigns the cast ages 6–10.
- **Wrong nationality/L1 — several inside assessed content:** "Carlos, whose first language is
  Russian" (6.1:37 + its Q4 anchor); "Fatima, whose first language is Cantonese" (6.3:54);
  "a Mandarin-speaking learner such as Fatima" (2.5:131, in the L1-errors quiz of all places);
  "Yuki, a student from Shanghai" (11.5:64, inside the unit's own anti-stereotyping example,
  propagating into Q5); 14.2 gives Fatima three identities (Polish "Fatima Kowalska",
  Spanish-speaking marketer) and makes Wei Japanese (body + Q6).
- **Mechanical artifacts:** "Wei-won" (1.1:21), "Daniel-ho" (11.4:64), "Priya-Carlos" (14.1:78),
  "São Wei" for São Paulo (1.4:49), "her groupmates Carlos and Fatima" where Fatima is also the
  subject (8.2:41), "Two other students, Fatima and Fatima" (12.4:129).
- **Wrong pronouns:** Mr. Osei as "She/Her" (5.1:122, 12.4:129, 13.3:129 ×3); Ms. Reyes "his"
  (5.2:69); Wei as "she/her" (5.3:19, 7.1:47, 10.1:55, module-02-test-key Q18).
- **Remap skipped/leaked:** 9.1 (the pilot) was never remapped — Maria, Paulo, Amara, Jin,
  Yusuf, Tomás all survive, one in quiz Q6. Out-of-cast leftovers elsewhere: Ben (3.3 incl.
  quiz; module-03-test Q8), Helen (3.6 incl. a distractor), Mateus (8.5 incl. a quiz anchor +
  module-08 key), Ajarn Noi (8.5), Amina (5.2, 10.6), Amira (11.4), Hana (12.1), Selin (14.2),
  Keiko (2.4), Farida (1.5).
- **Worst single case — 3.6 corrupts a real published source:** the unit now asserts that
  Dobson's *Dialogs for Everyday Use* Dialog 30 features "Yuki" opposite the original Helen — a
  false claim about a genuine State Department publication.
- **Invented cultural backstories** (11.5:50–52) pin shame-culture framing on specific national
  characters — exactly what the unit teaches against.

Also unfixed from the original review: vignette *density*. "A student/teacher named X" still
appears 493 times (6–15 per unit; cast.md's own rule says "a few per unit"), and the cast names
total ~1,370 mentions. The consolidation arguably made this tic more visible, not less.

### 2. Factual errors (short list, high stakes — several are quiz-keyed)

1. **1.1:5** — calls a learner in a US bilingual classroom an *EFL* learner while defining EFL
   as English-not-dominant countries. That's ESL. First unit, first section.
2. **4.1:47** — "The theorist whose work gave CLT its most widely cited intellectual framework
   was Stephen Krashen." Wrong: Hymes (communicative competence) → Wilkins → Canale & Swain;
   Krashen is the Natural Approach. None of the real names appears in the unit.
3. **4.5:43** — Kang, Sok & Han 2019 "analyzed … 428 studies": actual figure **54 studies /
   5,051 learners**; 428 is the article's start page hallucinated into a sample size. Stated
   twice, in the flagship evidence unit.
4. **4.5:47–80 + quiz Q5** — the claim that Kang et al. found the explicit–implicit gap "narrows
   on communicative/spontaneous measures" is not that paper's finding (they found larger effects
   on oral measures overall; implicit was more durable at delayed posttest). Reword or re-source.
5. **7.3:43 + quiz Q2** — Hamada 2020's adjustment called "publication bias" in the dialogue and
   quiz; it is **selection bias** via propensity scores — the unit's own body says so correctly.
6. **Pashler et al. year: 2009 → 2008** everywhere (unit 12.2 throughout, references, and
   **course-intro.md**, where the marketing pitch itself carries the wrong year).
7. **12.2:33/37/52 + quiz Q2** — the "variety of activity formats" prescription is attributed to
   Willingham, Hughes & Dobolyi (2015); the paper doesn't conclude that. Present it as the
   course's recommendation.
8. **2.4:24** — claims Spanish lacks a present-perfect equivalent (it has one: *haber* +
   participle) and that English "requires" *I have already eaten* over *I already ate* — the
   latter is standard **American** English; a US-market course is prescribing the British norm.
9. **2.5:73** — "Mandarin … [has] no direct grammatical equivalent of the progressive" — false
   (在/正在/着), and 2.3:79 says the opposite. Also 2.5:67 (Spanish/Italian "single past form")
   is wrong as stated, and 2.5:111's "Japanese infinitive after 'suggest'" reads invented.
10. **2.2:29 + quiz Q5** — adverb-placement rule ("before the subject or after the object")
    excludes standard mid-position ("She *often* reviews…") and the over-restriction is the
    keyed answer — the course certifies a false rule.
11. **Module 6 phonology is RP-based in an American-English course** — the big one. 6.1's vowel
    inventory (/ɒ/ "hot", /ɜː/ "bird", /əʊ/ "though"), 6.2's transcriptions (/ˈtiːtʃə/,
    /ˈkɜːnəl/, /dɒɡ/ — 12 lines after saying the course teaches rhotic American), 6.3's
    /aɪ wɒnt tuː ɡəʊ…/. 6.2's earlier "fix" only touched the framing. Needs a coordinated GA
    rewrite of 6.1–6.3, not spot edits. Also 6.1:33 "seven" voicing pairs omits /θ/–/ð/, and
    6.1:35's Arabic final-devoicing claim is wrong.
12. **5.3:19** — "word family" misdefined as words sharing a grammatical category (it's a base
    word + inflections/derivations), and the error structures the section.
13. **9.3:70** — Lyster & Saito's treatment-length finding linearized into "longer = larger
    gains" (their result was non-monotonic; Li 2010 found the opposite direction).
14. **7.2:15** — bottom-up processing equated with "rote memorization."
15. **9.2:11–15** — a recast labeled a prompt, in the module that teaches the distinction.
16. **Doctrine clashes:** 2.3 teaches "a grid of eight" while 2.4 teaches twelve forms and a
    3×4 grid (adjacent units certify opposite answers); 12.2 says stations assign every student
    to every station and keys a quiz on it, while 11.2/11.5/12.1/12.4 say students *choose*
    stations; 12.4 uses "aural/visual learners" language two units after 12.2 debunks it.

### 5. Unit-quiz anchors are unreliable (assessments' anchors are clean)

Across the five unit batches, roughly **45–50 of 378 unit-quiz anchors are not verbatim**, and
several are outright fabricated sentences quoted as "the unit states" (1.3 ×3, 2.3 Q2, 6.3 Q6
inventing "quiet", 10.1 Q3 inventing a first-person quote, 14.1 Q1, 14.4 Q4, 4.1 Q1). Failure
signature: paraphrase + embellishment words ("exactly", "simply", "actually") — anchors were
generated from memory, not extracted. **The assessment pipeline verified anchors (275/275
verbatim); the unit pipeline did not.** Port that check into the unit gate.

### 6. Module-test MCQ answer distribution is badly skewed

Across 196 parseable MCQs in the module keys: **b = 62%, c = 35%, a = 2%, d = 1%** — always-
answer-"b" passes most Section A's. The keyed option is also strictly the longest in 69% of
items. Needs a shuffle pass + length trim. Related: no point-allocation rubrics for the 17
scenario keys (graders get a model answer but no 0–5/0–10 guidance); final scenario 65 is the
M8 scenario recycled and 66 largely the M13 one — only 64 genuinely combines modules as the
contract requires; the M6 Q14 / M7 Q13 Section B item is duplicated back-to-back; module-08
Q9/Q12 leak each other's answer.

## Root cause and process fixes

Two systemic lessons, both cheap to encode:

1. **The gate checks style, not truth.** All 63 units carry "GATE PASS" while containing every
   defect above, because the gate is dslop + AI-lexicon regexes + quiz format + option labels.
   Add scripted checks that would have caught most of this mechanically:
   - cast linter: role keyword within N words of a cast name ("teacher named <learner>"),
     age-bracket words near names, nationality/L1 terms vs cast.md, pronoun agreement,
     hyphenated/fused cast names, duplicate names in one sentence, out-of-cast proper names;
   - anchor verifier: every unit-quiz anchor must be a verbatim substring of the body (the
     assessments pipeline already does this — port it);
   - references check: `## References` must be non-empty, and every in-text (Author, year) must
     have an entry; ban internal filenames in prose;
   - BrE spell list + " ," punctuation;
   - MCQ answer-letter distribution + keyed-option length bias, per test and course-wide.
2. **Every post-polish generation phase reintroduced pre-polish defects** (assessments and 9.1
   have BrE spelling; anchors regressed). Polish passes must either run last or live in the gate
   so they apply to everything produced after them.

## Suggested fix order

1. **Scripted sweeps first** (highest volume, lowest risk): cast linter + hand repair of all
   flagged sites (the big one — module 10, 14.4, 14.2, 11.2, 14.1 worst); BrE spelling pass over
   assessments + stragglers; " ," punctuation; fused names; pronouns.
2. **The enumerated factual fixes** (§2 above — ~16 items, each a paragraph-level edit except
   Module 6).
3. **Module 6 GA rewrite** (6.1–6.3 vowel system + transcriptions), **3.6 regeneration**, 8.4
   correction-codes content, 10.3 worked example (or retitle), reliability section in M13,
   2.3/2.4 reconciliation.
4. **Anchor repair** across ~50 unit-quiz items (script finds them; fix = re-quote from body).
5. **References**: populate 14 empty sections (a pedagogical grammar for M2/M3 fixes 10 of
   them), add missing entries, purge filename leaks.
6. **Assessment layer**: shuffle answer letters + trim keyed-option length; replace final
   scenarios 65/66; fix the duplicated Section B item and M8 Q9/Q12 leak; add scenario rubrics;
   fix the 6 cast errors in tests/keys.
7. **Editorial thinning** (optional but valuable): cut vignette density toward cast.md's own
   rule, kill "the source states" framing, vary the staccato tic.
8. Re-run the (now stronger) gate over everything, including assessments and course-intro.

Steps 1–6 are the launch gate. Step 7 is what separates "fixed" from "feels human-written."

## Bottom line

The June review's headline ("read one unit at a time, the writing is genuinely good; the
problems are aggregate") remains true, but this pass adds two corrections to it. First, the
06-09 remap — the fix for the biggest aggregate problem — introduced the single largest defect
class now in the course, and it landed in assessed content. Second, there are real factual
errors (a short, fully enumerated list), which the June pass declared out of scope on the
strength of citation verification alone; the citations are indeed excellent, but correctness
lives in more places than references. The product's bones — evidence honesty, structure,
assessment design, prose register — are genuinely strong and worth finishing properly.
