# Starting an online TEFL course: accreditation economics & seed material

Deep-research report, 2026-06-07. 24 sources fetched, 106 claims extracted, top 25 adversarially verified (3 independent votes each): 24 confirmed, 1 killed. Vendor-stated figures are authoritative for their own pricing but self-reported — re-check at decision time.

## The bottom line

Two accreditation tiers exist, and the gap between them is the whole story:

| | Pay-to-play (e.g. ITEFLAC) | Regulated (Ofqual Level 5 via TQUK) |
|---|---|---|
| Year-1 cost | **~$750** ($250 + $500 on award) | **£1,500** centre recognition + **£3,000/yr** (non-UK centres flat; UK centres waived once per-learner cert spend ≥ £3,000) |
| Process | Document-only, **no on-site visit, no classroom observation** | Six recognition requirements: qualified staff, facilities, QA procedures, assessment security, data protection, qualified Internal Quality Assurer (Level 4 IQA award) |
| Turnaround | **As little as 30 days** | Months (no verified figure for first-cohort timeline) |
| What it buys | A badge. Industry consensus (eslbase, TEFL Org, dotefl, teast): "Accredited by XYZ" alone guarantees nothing — some providers literally found their own accreditor | The same RQF level descriptor as CELTA/Trinity CertTESOL (Level 5; note: ~20 credits, not the 240 of a Foundation Degree — level comparison, not scope) |

Who actually uses what: i-to-i's Level 5 courses are genuinely Ofqual-regulated via **Gatehouse Awards** (Ofqual code 603/3955/X); International TEFL Academy runs Level 5 via **TQUK**; Bridge advertises only "Ofqual level *equivalency*" (regulated by ACCET/AQUEDUTO, not Ofqual — watch that wording trick). TEFL Course Review's legitimacy test (government registry listing + public provider list, or Ofqual/US-DoE/CHEA recognition) passes only 13 accreditors: ACTDEC, BAC, British Council, Cambridge, IATQuO, ODLQC, Qualifi, SQA, TQUK, Trinity (UK); ACCET, DEAC (US); TESL Canada. ACCREDITAT, ALAP, and "World TEFL Accrediting Commission" (now closed/merged into ACCRIN) appear nowhere on it.

## Free, legal course material (the build-don't-buy seed corpus)

- **"Shaping the Way We Teach English"** (US State Dept, americanenglish.state.gov) — a complete **14-module video teacher-training course** with manual, readings, and printable support materials. Free PDF/EPUB/MOBI download, no auth. Part of a ~1,000-resource library (Create to Communicate, Dialogs for Everyday Use, The Lighter Side of TEFL, Color Vowel Chart…).
- **Legal basis**: State Dept content is **public domain unless copyright indicated** (17 U.S.C. §105 + State's copyright notice). **VOA Learning English** texts/MP3s/videos are explicitly public domain **including commercial reuse** with attribution.
- ⚠️ The one real legal task: **screen out embedded third-party media** (AP/Reuters photos, licensed audio/video inside the materials) before commercial reuse.

## The SeriousTEFL model (verified in market)

$135 (or 3×$45) 120-hour automated diploma, **zero accreditation**, openly disparaging it ("membership based payments... no legitimate oversight"), substituting **their own job board** as the value proposition. Their claimed 12k partner schools / 48k alumni / "99% acceptance" are unverified vendor puffery — but the model itself is live and coherent: distribution beats credentials at the budget end.

## Killed claim (do not repeat)

"In the UK anyone can set up a company for £50 + three IDs and call it an accreditation body; most accreditors are pay-to-play" — refuted 0–3 as overreach. The narrower true version: *some* providers self-accredit, and a badge alone is meaningless.
