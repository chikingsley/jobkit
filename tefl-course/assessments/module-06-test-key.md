**1. Which of the following correctly describes how bilabial consonants are produced?**

   a. The tongue tip makes contact with the ridge of gum just behind the upper teeth.
   b. Both lips press together and then release, or redirect air through the nose.
   c. The back of the tongue rises to meet the soft palate at the rear of the mouth.
   d. The lower lip makes contact with the upper front teeth to create friction.

   *Answer: b. Both lips press together and then release, or redirect air through the nose.*
   *Anchor: "When both lips press together and then release, the result is either the voiceless sound at the start of 'pen' or the voiced sound at the start of 'bag'"*

**2. According to the unit, what distinguishes vowel production from consonant production in English?**

   a. Vowels are always voiced, whereas consonants can be either voiced or voiceless.
   b. Vowels are determined solely by lip rounding, while consonants depend on tongue position.
   c. Vowels are produced with the airway open throughout, while consonants involve some form of obstruction.

   *Answer: c. Vowels are produced with the airway open throughout, while consonants involve some form of obstruction.*
   *Anchor: "a vowel is produced with the airway open throughout. The tongue, the lips, and the jaw shape the resonating space inside the mouth"*

## Section B — Review (2 points, 1 each)

**13. Explain the grammatical difference between 'say' and 'tell' as reporting verbs, and give one correct example sentence for each.**

   *Answer: 'Say' takes a that-clause and never takes a personal object directly before the clause (e.g., 'She said that she was tired'). 'Tell' always requires a personal object placed directly before the clause (e.g., 'She told me that she was tired').*
   *Anchor: "unit 3.3 quiz"*

## Section C — Scenario (5 points)

**15. Mr. Osei is running a speaking activity in which learners take turns describing a memorable experience. Carlos is mid-sentence, telling the class about a childhood trip, when he mispronounces the vowel in 'ship' as the vowel in 'sheep.' The rest of the class — Daniel, Wei, Fatima, Yuki, and Priya — all nod along and show no sign of confusion. Mr. Osei is tempted to stop Carlos immediately and drill the correct vowel. What should Mr. Osei do, and why?**

   *Answer: Mr. Osei should allow Carlos to finish speaking without interrupting him. The module establishes that the practical standard for pronunciation teaching is intelligibility, not accent accuracy. In this scenario, every other learner in the room — Daniel, Wei, Fatima, Yuki, and Priya — is following Carlos's meaning without difficulty, which means the mispronounced vowel is not causing a breakdown in communication. Because intelligibility is intact, the module's position is that the teacher 'can ignore the issue of pronunciation altogether if student pronunciation is not interfering with communication,' and that classroom time is better directed toward activities that build fluency, vocabulary, or confidence. Beyond the time-allocation argument, the module warns explicitly that interrupting a student mid-utterance to correct a minor sound 'may have the negative effect of undermining student's self-confidence or preventing the development of oral fluency.' Carlos is engaged in a cognitively demanding task — selecting vocabulary, managing grammar, and sustaining a narrative — and stopping him to address a vowel that is causing no communicative obstacle breaks exactly the kind of extended spoken practice that builds fluency. If Mr. Osei judges that the /ɪ/–/iː/ distinction is worth addressing for Carlos's longer-term development, the appropriate moment is after the activity ends, when Carlos's confidence and the flow of the class have been preserved.*
   *Anchor: "can ignore the issue of pronunciation altogether if student pronunciation is not interfering with communication"*
