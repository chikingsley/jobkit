## Section C — Scenario (5 points)

**21. Mr. Osei is observing a lesson taught by a trainee teacher. Before the lesson, the trainee submitted this written aim: 'Students will do a flashcard activity and then complete a mind map about personality adjectives.' During the lesson, Mr. Osei watches Carlos, Daniel, Wei, Fatima, Yuki, and Priya complete both tasks without difficulty. At the end of the session, Mr. Osei asks the trainee, 'How do you know whether your students learned anything today?' The trainee is unable to answer. If you were the trainee, how would you have written the aim differently, and why does the distinction matter for assessing whether the lesson succeeded?**

   *Answer: The trainee's original statement describes two classroom activities rather than a learning outcome, so it cannot answer the question Mr. Osei poses. A statement such as 'students will do a flashcard activity and then complete a mind map' tells you what Carlos, Daniel, Wei, Fatima, Yuki, and Priya will do during the lesson, not what they will be able to do when it is over. Even if every task is completed smoothly, no evidence of durable learning follows automatically from task completion. A revised aim should use outcome-focused language built around the formula 'learners will be able to…' followed by a verb that names a cognitive demand. Drawing on Bloom's Taxonomy, the trainee might distinguish between recognition and production: if the goal is production, the verb 'use' is more precise than 'identify.' A stronger aim might read: 'Students will be able to use six personality adjectives accurately in original spoken sentences describing a character.' That formulation names the words, the cognitive demand, and the context. It also makes assessment possible: at the close of the lesson, Mr. Osei or the trainee can ask Yuki or Priya to describe a character using one of the day's adjectives and immediately judge whether the objective was met. The original aim offers no such measure; a lesson in which students completed every planned task may still have produced no durable learning at all. The activities—flashcards and the mind map—are not discarded; they become stages that serve the stated outcome rather than ends in themselves.*
   *Anchor: "a lesson in which students completed every planned task may still have produced no durable learning at all"*
   *Rubric (5 points): identifies that the submitted statement describes activities rather than a learning outcome (2); reframes it as an assessable aim stating what learners will be able to do (2); explains why the aim-versus-activity distinction matters for the lesson (1).*
