training center contracts that pencil out. The best positions are in public

through the manual, please let me know what you're thinking and we can go
from there.

quality of offers as they'd like because their resumes are off. You can
upgrade your package if desired within the first 30 days and we can work on
getting all of your documents looking professional and polished.

offering in terms of hourly compensation. I look forward to continuing this
conversation with you.

Hi Chibuzor,

I've gone over your resume in detail. Please understand that this critique
is written with your applying to ESL positions overseas in mind. Feel free
to implement what you like and disregard what you don't. It's of course
ultimately your document.

That said, you have hired me to do this review and give you unbiased and
direct feedback, so I will not hold back in any area I think you can
improve.

When I first open a resume, I like 5 things to jump out at me immediately
before scrolling down. These are:

1.) Native level speaker
2.) Degrees
3.) Certs
4.) Skype
5.) Availability

These are the five pieces of data that most everyone looking through
resumes is seeking.  According to Statistic Brain, the human attention span
is 8 seconds, which is 1 second shorter than a goldfish, so it's especially
important that this information be easy to read.

Starting from the top, I would:

1.) put USA after WV

2.) put a +1 in front of my phone number

3.) consider listing a mailing address

These three items above lend credibility and remind the reader subtly that
you have a home in the US and you are an American and native speaker.

I am a big proponent of creating summary statements that immediately grab
the attention of the reader. I like to let them know within 2 seconds of
opening our document why they need to give us the interview. Instead of
telling the reader what we're looking for, I like to show what we have to
offer. I'd consider creating a bold statement between your contact details
and education sections that shows what you bring to the table. Based on
what I'm reading here, it might be something like:

*TEFL-Certified Professional Educator with Bachelor's Degree and American
Passport Available Immediately *

^^If I were looking for a qualified teacher and read this upon opening your
resume, you'd absolutely have my attention. You show right off the bat that
you check all the boxes of the average employer abroad.

Education section:

I'd list USA after WV. Many people are unfamiliar with US cities and
states. It's totally up to you if you want to list graduation dates, but I
would not here. It will bring attention to the fact that you're a recent
graduate. I wouldn't deny it, but I just wouldn't highlight it. I would
drop the GPA as well.

Professional Experience:

I would put USA after all of these city and state combos.

My honest feeling is that we should seriously consider dropping the jobs
that have really short durations. I have been beaten up in interviews so
many times because of 3-6 month jobs that I have deleted them from my
resume over the years. Why?

Employers are trained to look for red flags just like we look for red flags
with them. They look for a few things in ESL:

1.) Very short duration employment. This could potentially show you're a
flight risk. Many people list every short term position they've ever had
and employers say "They are going to leave after 3 months. Too risky. We
don't want to invest in the visa and getting him over here if there's a
high likelihood he won't last." They often gloss over these too... and I'd
hate for them to dismiss your resume without realizing what were you doing
at all these jobs back to back.

2.) Listing a lot of intern and tutoring jobs. Many people pad their
resumes with this because it's often unverifiable experience. I have
dropped this from my resume because I got tired of explaining myself--just
like with short duration employment. I'm sure countless others scrapped my
resume without allowing me to explain.

3.) Frequently changing locations. This brings about concern for obvious
reasons.

Your Evoqua experience is solid. It's a year and a half in duration and
it's obvious you did a lot. If you had any experience training,
instructing, working with people whose native language was not English,
etc... I would go into that in more detail.

The one time I would seriously consider keeping this intern experience in
here is if I were applying to specific Safety English programs like with
Saudi Aramco or something similar. In that case, it could be useful to show
you've had the training.

Research Experience:

This looks great.

Skills:

It's up to you if you want to leave this on here or not. If you're planning
to teach computer stuff, this could be useful. For ESL, I think honestly
that most people won't know these programs and acronyms.

Your activities look great. The only thing to point out is that under
Mountaineer United, you begin to use punctuation in your final two bullets.
I would delete it so that these match the format of the rest of your
resume.

### 2019-08-28 — Ben (ben@benteachesenglishoverseas.com)
*Subject: Re: Job Search Update + Feedback requested*

Hi Chi,

It's great to hear from you.

There are a couple options right now in China to earn great money:

1.) Get on with a top notch international school. This is hard if you're
just starting out.

2.) Get on with a job that has very few hours but decent pay and then
augment your income by adding on extra employment at great hourly rates.

So is 16k common? Absolutely. Is it the best you can do? No.

Most important is to take the entire contract and calculate exactly what
you'll be earning per hour. From there you can compare apples to apples.

For example, if it were 16k for 25 teaching hours that were all together
and no office hours, you'd be making a LOT more.. even with the same 16k. I
hope that makes sense. You could then use those extra hours to do something
else to make more money instead of sitting in the office.

I find training centers often have the highest sticker price, but when it
comes to calculation, they come in the lowest per hour because of the
amount of time they expect you to be on site each week.

Your questions:

1.) ESLcafe is going to list almost exclusively training centers. I would
look more at expat specific website job sections like shenzhenparty.com for
Shenzhen. I would be looking at seriousteachers.com. I would be talking to
head hunters on Skype and letting them know exactly what you're looking
for. I'd look at Angelina's ESL cafe. I'd stay away from Dave's.

2.) I find that emails without questions are easier to ignore. I like to
ask at least one open ended question that requires a response. Many people
feel obligated to respond and then it lets me know that the email is
active, they received my documents, and my message went to the right place.
Then I can target follow ups. This could be as simple as: Is your school
still currently seeking an English teacher for the fall term?

This might not work for everyone, but it has worked well for me in the
past. I also frequently follow up.

3.) No, it's not... but again it's best to look at the overall package. Is
16k net great for 50 hour weeks? No way. Is it great for 10 hours a week I
can pack all into a Saturday? You bet.

Ben
