# JobKit: Five Real Exits

Status: Focused product direction

Last updated: 2026-07-17

Current canonical ESL scope: [`jobkit-product-prd.md`](jobkit-product-prd.md)

Recruiting business and licensing: [`recruiting-business/`](recruiting-business/)

## Product direction

JobKit helps someone leave for a place where they can begin a workable next chapter. The first product centers on international ESL work because the experience, source inventory, qualification logic, outreach system, and application executors already exist.

The product starts with a direct question:

> Where do you want to go?

JobKit turns the answer into a small set of current routes, completes the application work with the candidate, tracks every external action, and carries the selected route through interview, offer review, and departure preparation.

The product promise is:

> Five real exits, built around your passport, qualifications, money, and departure date.

## Launch scope

The launch has two route types:

1. international ESL employment;
1. WWOOF or Workaway.info footholds that provide an initial place to stay.

The bartending experience supplies the quality standard for future categories: bounded preparation, employer demand attached to the training, named opportunities, visible fill status, and a practical refresher path.

## International ESL employment

ESL is the core business. JobKit already has the strongest understanding of the market, the qualifications, the application materials, the source platforms, and the country-level differences.

For each candidate, JobKit should produce five current routes. Each route includes:

- a named school, program, or recruiter;
- a current vacancy or confirmed outreach destination;
- country and city;
- salary, hours, housing, airfare, visa support, and contract length;
- candidate eligibility with source evidence;
- missing documents and the exact way to obtain them;
- an application packet ready for approval;
- an interview and departure timeline;
- the latest verified state of the application; and
- a fallback destination.

The workflow is concrete:

1. The candidate chooses countries, departure window, income floor, and living constraints.
1. JobKit resolves passport, degree, experience, license, and document eligibility.
1. JobKit returns five named routes with current evidence.
1. The candidate selects routes and approves each external action.
1. JobKit prepares and executes the applications through the correct channel.
1. JobKit verifies the result at the destination and tracks replies, interviews, offers, and follow-ups.
1. JobKit compares offers and builds the visa, travel, housing, and arrival checklist.

The first useful service can be high-touch. A candidate pays for a completed route package and ongoing execution support. The software captures the repeated work as the process becomes predictable.

## WWOOF and Workaway.info footholds

[WWOOF](https://wwoof.net/howitworks/) and [Workaway.info](https://www.workaway.info/en/info/how-it-works/workawayer) can provide a temporary landing place through cultural exchange. A host typically provides accommodation and meals in exchange for agreed help. The immediate value is a destination, a bed, local contact, and time to get oriented.

This route begins with the same question:

> Where do you want to land, and how long do you need a foothold?

JobKit can handle the preparation and coordination:

- build the candidate's profile;
- select countries, dates, host types, and acceptable work;
- shortlist active hosts with recent reviews and response activity;
- prepare personalized host messages;
- coordinate candidate-approved outreach;
- confirm dates, daily hours, tasks, room, meals, internet, transport, and arrival instructions in writing;
- secure a backup host;
- verify passport, entry, insurance, and travel requirements; and
- connect the foothold to an ESL employment plan where the candidate is eligible.

The deliverable is a written landing plan with a confirmed host, exact arrival instructions, the full exchange terms, a backup, and the next income step.

## The bartending standard

The bartending experience matters as a product pattern. A short, bounded preparation period led into an active employer network where specific jobs appeared, candidates could act, and filled jobs visibly closed.

A future route earns a place in JobKit when it demonstrates:

1. a bounded preparation task with a predictable time and cost;
1. a clear completion standard that an employer or provider recognizes;
1. named employer demand attached to the completed preparation;
1. current opportunities with visible status;
1. a direct application or placement path;
1. evidence of interviews, hires, and completed work; and
1. a refresher path when skills expire.

A proven provider-to-candidate handoff is the research gate for every future category. Promising occupations stay in the research backlog until manual proof exists.

## Route evidence standard

Every published route needs a current evidence packet:

- provider identity and authoritative source;
- live vacancy, host availability, or confirmed outreach destination;
- last verified date;
- eligibility rules and supporting source;
- exact application channel and contact;
- expected response timeline;
- all candidate expenses before departure;
- compensation or exchange terms;
- housing and food terms;
- passport, visa, and work-authorization requirements;
- contract or commitment length;
- safety and arrival contacts;
- current workflow state; and
- authoritative evidence for submission, acceptance, and placement.

The route card should expose these facts directly. Research gaps remain in the internal queue until the evidence packet is complete.

## Candidate experience

The candidate sees one next action at a time:

1. complete the passport, qualification, budget, and document profile;
1. choose the destination, income, housing, schedule, and departure constraints;
1. review five named routes with current evidence and exact next actions;
1. approve each employer message, application, and document packet;
1. prepare for interviews with a route-specific session and practice questions;
1. select an offer or confirmed foothold; and
1. complete the departure checklist.

Human support handles momentum, judgment, and accountability. The software handles qualification, document preparation, repeatable outreach, state tracking, reminders, and proof.

## Naming

Use **JobKit: Five Real Exits** for the pilot. It explains the product while preserving the existing JobKit identity.

Use **Night Mover** as a possible name for the high-touch concierge service after the pilot proves the delivery model.

Suggested lockup:

> **JobKit: Five Real Exits.** Choose where you want to go. We build the route with you.

## Advertising

### Primary message

> **Where do you want to go?** Tell us your passport, qualifications, budget, and departure window. We will build five current routes and help you complete one.

### Supporting messages

- Choose a country, get a real route, and complete it with help.
- Five current destinations, each with the employer or host, costs, requirements, and next action already mapped.
- Start with a job or a place to land, then arrive with a plan.

### Proof content

Publish individual route dossiers showing the named destination, candidate requirements, current opportunity, application path, costs, housing, timeline, and verified outcome. Each dossier becomes both product inventory and advertising evidence.

The founder story should focus on the empowering handoff: learn a bounded skill, receive concrete opportunities, act while the opening is live, and see the outcome.

## Pilot

Run a high-touch pilot with ten candidates.

### ESL track

- build each candidate's complete qualification profile;
- deliver five current, named routes within seven days;
- execute candidate-approved applications;
- track interviews, offers, and departure progress; and
- record every research gap and manual intervention.

### Foothold track

- build the WWOOF or Workaway.info profile;
- produce ten host candidates in the selected region;
- execute candidate-approved outreach;
- obtain written terms from responsive hosts;
- secure a primary and backup landing plan; and
- connect the arrival plan to an eligible ESL route.

### Success measures

- completed profiles;
- routes that pass the evidence standard;
- candidate-approved external actions;
- verified submissions;
- interviews and host acceptances;
- employment offers;
- confirmed departures; and
- days from intake to each milestone.

The pilot should reveal the true workload, the strongest customer promise, and the exact automation worth building next.
