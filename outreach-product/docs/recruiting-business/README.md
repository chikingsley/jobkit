# JobKit recruiting business

Status: Proposed pilot

Last updated: 2026-07-17

## The business

JobKit can sell one completed outcome from three directions:

- a school buys a teacher who is screened, document-ready, and prepared to interview;
- a candidate buys training, application execution, and relocation support; and
- a licensed local recruiter buys a prepared candidate pipeline.

The same operating system serves all three. The candidate profile, eligibility engine, job inventory, application packet, outreach history, interview preparation, and departure checklist already live inside the product.

The model is credible credential, confidential workflow, and employer-funded placement.

## The words

A **hagwon** is a Korean private academy. English hagwons are after-school businesses that hire a large share of Korea's foreign English teachers.

A **licensed recruiter** is a placement business authorized by a government employment authority. The authorization usually covers paid introductions between employers and candidates, fee collection, records, privacy, advertising, and complaint handling. Each country sets its own rules. A license proves regulatory permission. Placement history, screening quality, and replacement performance prove commercial value.

Established agencies already buy outside sourcing. Chinese recruiters publish referral payouts and retention triggers, a Thai agency publicly invites overseas recruitment partners, Japan publishes a formal overseas-intermediary structure, and EPIK uses approved overseas agencies. Public partner splits are scarce, which makes a 30–50% JobKit sourcing share the first direct-sales test.

## Launch decision

Launch through licensed destination partners while JobKit proves demand and unit economics.

1. **Korea:** first regulated-market pilot. The public fee benchmark is about $980-$1,306 per teacher, and JobKit currently holds 88 active listings across 37 named companies.
1. **Thailand:** first direct-school pilot. JobKit currently holds 215 active listings across 147 named companies, including 158 rows with application email addresses.
1. **China:** first volume partner channel. JobKit currently holds 561 active listings, including 503 rows with application email addresses. Local recruiters control much of the visible supply.
1. **Japan:** licensed-partner pilot after Korea. JobKit currently holds 75 active listings across 44 named companies.
1. **Colombia:** first Latin American commercial test. Bilingual and international schools support higher salaries and employer-paid recruiting.
1. **Georgia and Spain:** defined later products built around sponsor-ready schools and formal assistant programs.

Kazakhstan and Brazil belong to the qualified-school lane. Russia stays on the research desk because travel, consular, banking, and sanctions exposure create an unusually heavy operating burden.

## The first school offer

> Give us the role, salary, visa constraints, and start date. Within ten business days, we deliver three screened, interview-ready candidates. You pay $1,000 when your selected teacher starts. The placement carries a 60-day replacement credit.

Pilot payment terms:

- $500 at signed offer;
- $500 on the teacher's first working day; and
- one replacement search credited during the first 60 days.

For Korea, Japan, China, and Colombia, a licensed local partner signs the placement agreement and bills the school. JobKit receives a contracted sourcing and preparation share. The first proposed split gives the local partner 60% and JobKit 40% of collected placement revenue. China, Japan, and Colombia then move to the market-specific price card in [`offers-and-sales.md`](offers-and-sales.md).

The seven-day service sells departure execution. Employment follows the employer and immigration calendar. Entry requires a current passport, a lawful entry route, a 90-day living runway, and an emergency return reserve.

Candidate payments purchase named training, preparation, technology, privacy, and relocation deliverables. School payments purchase recruitment. Separate agreements, invoices, and accounting preserve that line.

## Brand architecture

**ESL JobKit** is the literal product: find jobs, qualify, prepare, apply, and track.

**Night Mover by JobKit** is the managed service: choose where to land, build the route, and carry it through departure.

**JobKit for Schools** is the employer offer: receive three screened candidates and pay on a successful start.

The product can speak in four voices:

- **Adventure:** “Pick a country. We will build the route.”
- **Stuck:** “You need to get the fuck out. Give us 90 days.”
- **Urgent:** “Leave safely this week. Land with 90 days of runway.”
- **Confidential:** “Move with a small circle. Use Signal, neutral notifications, limited staff access, and timed data deletion.”

## How schools become clients

The first outreach starts with current demand already visible in JobKit.

1. Rank employers by repeated active vacancies, start date, direct contact, salary, and visa support.
1. Prepare three candidates who fit the employer's current role, salary, visa, and start-date pattern.
1. Contact the hiring manager with those candidate files and a ten-day delivery promise.
1. Ask about annual hiring volume, peak months, visa rules, present agency fee, payment trigger, replacement term, and recurring failure points.
1. Offer one success-fee pilot tied to a signed offer and completed start.
1. Convert a successful placement into a semester or annual supply agreement.

The current inventory already exposes immediate partner prospects: Embark Recruiting, Cube Recruiting, PlanetESL, ANESL, Gold Star TEFL Recruitment, MediaKids Academy Thailand, Thai Singapore International School, and Kajonkietsuksa School.

## Thirty-day validation

1. Recruit ten candidates: five Korea-eligible graduates, two China candidates, two Thailand candidates, and one Colombia candidate.
1. Interview five licensed agencies and ten direct schools about fees, triggers, shortages, and replacement terms.
1. Present the school offer with a real candidate slate and destination-specific readiness files.
1. Secure two signed pilot agreements with a defined fee, payment trigger, and replacement allocation.
1. Run twenty interviews and reach one paid start.
1. Record hours, acquisition cost, partner split, failure reasons, and days between each milestone.

The licensing threshold arrives after proof: fifty annual placements in one regulated destination, predictable school demand, and a local operator capable of running the office. Until that point, partner fees buy speed, local trust, and regulatory coverage.
