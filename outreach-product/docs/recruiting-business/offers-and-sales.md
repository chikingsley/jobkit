JobKit's price therefore depends on completed screening, legal readiness, interviews, arrival, and retention.

## Three recruiter businesses

### Direct placement

The school employs the teacher, and the recruiter receives a one-time success fee. Direct placement leads the JobKit business because it has simple unit economics and familiar market terms.

### Overseas sourcing partner

JobKit supplies screened candidates to a licensed local agency. The agency owns school intake, local introduction, immigration handoff, invoicing, and collection. JobKit receives a sourcing fee after the teacher starts and reaches the agreed retention trigger.

The overseas sourcing partnership is the first operating model for Korea, China, Thailand, Japan, and Colombia.

### Staffing and dispatch

The agency employs or manages the teacher and invoices the school for a continuing service. The spread funds payroll, permits, supervision, curriculum, substitutes, support, and replacements.

Japan's ALT dispatch market and Thailand's procured foreign-teacher services prove the model. China requires a separate labor-dispatch license and CNY 2 million paid-in capital. Staffing becomes a later company after recurring placements establish school volume.

The standard school pitch is:

> Give us the role, salary, visa constraints, and start date. We deliver three screened, interview-ready candidates in ten business days. You pay when your selected teacher starts. The placement includes early-retention coverage.

The first Korea pilot can use a discounted ₩750,000 fee, about $500, after start. The discount buys a documented case study and a reference. The standard rate returns to ₩1.5 million after proof.

## What “screened” means

Every candidate slate should carry the same evidence:

- identity and nationality;
- degree, license, TEFL, and experience records;
- destination-specific visa lane;
- authentication and criminal-check status;
- compensation, location, age group, schedule, housing, and start-date fit;
- live interview scorecard;
- reference review;
- short recorded teaching demonstration;
- document completion date;
- candidate commitment and availability; and
- named JobKit operator.

That package is the product. A resume is one item inside it.

The credential offer should state what the candidate receives: instructional hours, live sessions, observed teaching, assessment standard, issuer, authentication path, and destination acceptance history. China candidates use an issuer with a proven work-permit document trail. Korea candidates use a course format that meets the chosen program's published requirement. Thailand candidates pair training with the degree and school-specific permit lane.

## Planning economics

The partner model begins with modest placement contribution and strong candidate-service contribution.

Example Korea start:

- school fee: $1,000;
- local partner share at 60%: $600;
- JobKit placement revenue: $400;
- candidate Readiness revenue: $1,250 when independently purchased;
- managed candidate service revenue: $2,000 when independently purchased; and
- direct travel and document expenses billed separately.

The same candidate can therefore generate revenue from distinct contracts while every party receives a named deliverable. Early interviews should test willingness to pay before automation work expands.

## School acquisition

### Start with candidates

An abstract “recruiting service” pitch asks the school to imagine future value. A prepared candidate slate shows the value immediately.

Opening message:

> We have three candidates available for your next intake. Each has a verified qualification file, document timeline, destination-fit interview, teaching sample, and confirmed start window. Send us the role and package; we will return the matching files and coordinate interviews.

### Use visible hiring repetition

JobKit already records repeated vacancies, current contacts, start dates, and application routes. Rank prospects by active vacancy count, repeated semesters, direct hiring contact, visa and housing support, salary clarity, multiple campuses, candidate complaints, and retention history.

### Use each market's natural channel

- **Korea:** licensed teacher recruiters, the national registered-academy dataset, hagwon franchise headquarters, and repeat advertisers.
- **China:** recruiter referral programs, school-owned vacancy pages, universities, international-school groups, and existing ANESL contacts.
- **Thailand:** Department of Employment agency register, Ajarn repeat advertisers, direct school HR, English Program heads, and public procurement calendars.
- **Japan:** licensed teacher agencies, private language-school groups, preschools, bilingual schools, and private secondary schools.
- **Colombia:** authorized placement partners, MingaTeachers employers, bilingual schools, international schools, and AMISA members.

### Interview questions

Ask every school about annual foreign-teacher volume, peak months, long-open roles, document delays, immigration failures, current agency fee, payment trigger, replacement period, costly departures, and the final hiring and visa approvers.

Ask every licensed agency about high-demand candidates, license coverage, the required sourcing file, fee trigger, JobKit share, replacement allocation, candidate communication, candidate attribution, data permissions, and a three-candidate semester pilot.

## First prospect sprint

Week one:

- contact five Korean agencies;
- contact two Chinese agencies with public referral programs;
- verify and contact one Thai licensed agency;
- contact five repeated Thai schools; and
- contact three Japanese teacher agencies.

Week two:

- send three candidate files to every responsive partner;
- collect exact partner fees and triggers;
- revise the contracts and price card; and
- close one school or agency pilot.

The missing fact is narrow and testable: the amount each licensed agency will pay JobKit for a candidate who begins work and remains through the first retention checkpoint.
