# "What's your knowledge of MEP systems?"

## Say it like this

Yeah, MEP coordination's been a big part of my work, MEP being mechanical, electrical, and plumbing. On my Pfizer warehouse retrofit the mechanical scope basically drove the whole job, because it was pharma storage, so we needed tight temperature control with redundancy and monitoring on it. On top of that there was the electrical service for the building, and the fire sprinkler design for the high-piled racking. So I've coordinated those trades and the MEP design against the permits and against the schedule. And honestly a pharma facility is a tougher MEP problem than a standard building, because a lot of those systems have to be formally validated before you can use them, which is a higher bar than a normal inspection.

## Notes

- MEP = Mechanical (HVAC), Electrical (power, lighting, fire alarm), Plumbing (water, sewer, gas, plus fire sprinkler).
- Never say "no familiarity." You have a stronger MEP story than most candidates because pharma temperature control is harder than a typical building.
- If pushed on specifics: temperature bands (CRT 15–25°C, cold room 2–8°C), HVAC redundancy, and that the systems get validated (IQ/OQ/PQ), not just inspected.
