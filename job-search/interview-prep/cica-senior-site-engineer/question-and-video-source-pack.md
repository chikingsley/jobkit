# Cica Interview Question and Video Source Pack

This document separates evidence from practice material. A question can be useful without pretending that Cica has asked it before.

## Provenance key

- **Reported Cica:** A candidate says Cica asked the question. The reports are from Taiwan and may involve a different role or interviewer.
- **Role-derived:** The prompt was written from a duty in the attached Senior Site Engineering Supervisor job description.
- **Comparable-role:** The prompt comes from the work described in a live gas, chemical, or semiconductor-facilities role at another employer.
- **Technical drill:** The prompt checks a concept needed to discuss the role. It carries no claim about an employer's interview process.

- How do you integrate commissioning into the construction schedule?
- What tells you a system is ready for startup?
- How do you handle a field change with design, contractor, and customer input?

## Video curriculum

Watch the first three before chasing more material. Close the video, give a one-minute explanation in your own words, then answer the prompt. The goal is to absorb the environment and vocabulary while keeping your own project history separate from the case on screen.

### 1. Start with the full project environment

[Commissioning a Chip Manufacturing Plant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VD2pOILI4sg) is a 58-minute 2025 case discussion from AABC, ACG, and EMA. It covers early commissioning involvement, contractor and vendor schedules, chemical skids, partial turnover, startup risk, field quality walks, and the gap between an operating system and a fully commissioned system.

After watching, answer:

> Where should commissioning enter the project, and what breaks when the team treats it as an end-of-construction activity?

Then connect one idea to a recent Seagen or Pfizer project. Keep the case study and your own experience clearly separated.

### 2. See the actual gas and chemical service model

[MEGASYS Gas and Chemical Delivery Systems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kAWxwEtoZkk) is a three-minute overview from EMD Electronics. It shows the service setting, the safety emphasis, and the relationship between onsite teams and semiconductor customers.

After watching, answer:

> What would you need to learn during your first month before supervising work on one of these systems?

### 3. Learn the equipment vocabulary

[Air Products CHEMGUARD Chemical Delivery System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8wiqu1ZjHbY) is a four-minute equipment overview. Use it to identify the source container, cabinet, pressure or pumping arrangement, valves, instruments, containment, and delivery path.

After watching, draw the system from memory and answer:

> If flow disappears at the point of use, where would you establish the system boundary and what evidence would you gather first?

### 4. Practice drawing-to-field language

[How to Read a P&ID](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4EOTerfyTY) from RealPars is a short refresher on equipment, piping, instrumentation, and control relationships.

After watching, answer:

> What can you verify from the P&ID, and what still requires another drawing or a field walk?

### 5. Understand clean delivery and contamination

[Clean Chemical Delivery: Fluid Handling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LABcpIiun78) from Entegris is a two-minute view of contamination control across the fluid path.

After watching, answer:

> How could a seemingly minor component substitution affect purity, equipment, or wafer yield?

### 6. Separate construction completion from commissioning

[Pre-Commissioning vs Commissioning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YwNfsYsos1w) from the Institute of Commissioning and Assurance is an eight-minute overview of the boundary between installation checks and functional proof.

After watching, answer:

> What evidence would you want before allowing a hazardous chemical system to move from construction into live functional testing?

### 7. Add cleanroom qualification vocabulary

[Cleanroom Certification and Facility Qualification](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gXSP83JDp7c) is a short overview of particle counts, filter integrity, airflow, pressure, temperature, humidity, and qualification stages.

After watching, explain the difference between making the room ready for the specialist and personally owning the certification result.

### 8. Use the Arizona wastewater context when it helps

[Water Reuse and Semiconductor Wastewater Management](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hZ_PRkAA7RM) is a 2026 Arizona State University technical seminar. It is optional and much longer, but it connects semiconductor manufacturing, local water constraints, and waste-stream treatment.

After watching, use an older Dow or Evoqua example only if the interviewer asks about wastewater, treatment trains, clarifiers, or water reuse. State why that earlier example is the most direct one.

## One recording loop

1. Pick one reported or sourced question, then watch the relevant video segment or review the source duty.
1. Close the notes and answer for 60 to 90 seconds.
1. Mark each statement as personal experience, technical knowledge, or a scenario response.
1. Listen once for voice, then send the recording for an outside grade.

Grade each recording from zero to two on five dimensions:

- The answer begins with a position rather than a biography.
- The wording sounds spoken and specific to you.
- A recent example leads unless an older example has unique technical value.
- Ownership and technical claims can survive a follow-up question.
- The ending lands without another loop of explanation.

The next revision should remove whatever feels performed. More vocabulary can help, but a shorter answer you can defend is stronger than a complete answer you cannot inhabit.
