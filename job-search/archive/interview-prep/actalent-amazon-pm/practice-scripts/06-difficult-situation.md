# "Tell me about a difficult situation in construction." (dial into solutions, not problems)

## Say it like this

So one that comes to mind was a permitting issue with the city on my Pfizer retrofit. The short version of the problem is a permit item was threatening to hold up our schedule. What I did was take it on directly. I owned it through the city's permit portal, I set up a tight follow-up cadence with them so the submittal kept moving instead of just sitting in a queue, and I kept the stakeholders updated the whole way through. We got the issuance in time and it held the schedule. And honestly that's pretty much how I operate in general. When something goes wrong, I'd rather show up with the fix and a couple of options than just hand someone the problem.

## Notes

- They literally said "dial into solutions rather than problems," so keep the problem to one sentence and spend the rest on what you did.
- Backup story if they want a second one: the sprinkler-clearance fix after the Pfizer turnover (you ran a minor-works project to raise the heads back into NFPA clearance).
- End on the solutions-first line. That's the exact attitude they're testing for.
